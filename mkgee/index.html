reel 02 — origins

Six, then eleven

Piano lessons from around age six. Guitar picked up at eleven. Self-recording started not long after — years before there was any audience for it at all, and years before anyone would call what he was doing a "sound."

There was no studio. No engineer. No one telling him what a finished song was supposed to sound like. Just a bedroom, an instrument, and whatever gear happened to be within reach.

Everything that follows on this page — the pedals, the tape machine, the specific way the mix sits — traces back to this. Hours alone with an instrument, long before any gear made it sound like anything in particular.

reel 03 — 2017

2017

"I Know How You Get"

The debut single. Self-released, self-produced. No label, no rollout machine — just a bedroom project finding its first listeners.

It didn't sound like a demo. That was the point, and it's the first sign of a pattern that holds for the next six years: the limitations weren't hidden, they were the sound.

reel 04 — 2018

On the way

Two self-produced EPs, Pronounced McGee and Fool, both made the same way the first single was: alone, with whatever was in the room. Then Frank Ocean played "You" on his Beats 1 radio show — the first sign this wasn't staying a bedroom secret, and the first outside confirmation that the limitations were working.

"Every era on this timeline starts the same way: alone, with a guitar and a tape machine."

reel 05 — the other side of the desk

Writing and producing for other artists

Dijon, Omar Apollo, Bon Iver, Justin Bieber among them. Proof the sound isn't a solo-project fluke: other people are hiring it, the same gear and the same instincts, just pointed at someone else's song.

This is the stretch most profiles skip, because nothing here is a headline. But it's also the stretch where the sound stopped being one person's accident and started being a method other people could call on.
